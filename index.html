The signal

A century-old discipline, deployed where it’s least crowded.

Entries and exits are generated by Ichimoku Kinko Hyo — literally “one-glance equilibrium chart” — a multi-horizon trend-confirmation framework developed by a Japanese journalist in the late 1930s and refined for three decades before publication. It demands several independent conditions agree on the SAME trading bar before a position is even considered: a momentum cross between two trend-tracking lines, price standing on the correct side of a forward-projected equilibrium band, and a confirmation check against price from well before that bar. The specific horizons and thresholds used here are proprietary and are not disclosed on this page — the discipline is.

The universe is deliberately single-stock NSE monthly futures, not the index: 212 names, re-ranked and re-selected every single trading day, capped by GICS sector so no one theme can dominate a day’s risk. Single-name futures see materially less systematic and high-frequency flow than index products or the largest cap-weighted names — exactly the corner of the market where a patient, rules-based trend signal still has room to work.

Why this belongs in a diversified book

Returns that don’t move with the market.

The hardest thing to find for a portfolio already long Indian equities, or running other systematic books, is a return stream that doesn’t secretly share the same risk. This is the case for it, measured the way any allocator would: a daily-return comparison against the NSE’s headline large-cap index, over the identical window selected above. The goal of a market-neutral futures program was never to out-earn a fully-invested equity index in a bull market — it is to do so with a fraction of the drawdown, and with a return stream that moves independently of it.

Overnight and intraday gaps — priced conservatively, not assumed away

A trailing stop is only ever hit at the level it was actually possible to trade at. When the market has already moved past that level before the next print — an overnight gap, or a sudden intraday move — the program never books the untouched, no-longer-achievable price. It uses the worse of the stop level or the real print that followed, as a floor that requires no further assumption. Wherever finer intraday data independently confirms it, that floor is refined further to the volume-weighted average price of the first few minutes of trading — which, tested against the simpler floor across the full backtest, comes out more conservative on average, not less: a gap tends to keep drifting for a few minutes before it stabilizes, rather than stopping dead at the first print.

Entry-fill sensitivity — a slower, more averaged fill

The base case fills every entry at the next bar's own open print — the earliest real, achievable price, but a single idealized tick. As a robustness check, every entry across the full 7+ year history was instead re-priced at the volume-weighted average price of that same bar's first five minutes — a slower, more averaged fill that no longer assumes a perfect execution at the very first print. The 0.10% impact-cost assumption already deducted throughout this page is layered on top of this test exactly as it is everywhere else, not removed — so the figures below combine two separate, independently conservative execution assumptions, not one. Signal generation, stop-loss and trailing-stop logic, and exit pricing are completely unchanged; only the entry price moves.

Trade signature

Wrong often, right big.

A trend-following program is not trying to be right on every trade — it is trying to be right on the trades that matter. A losing position is cut quickly by design; a winning one carries no fixed target and is held — and added to — for as long as the trend itself continues.

Out-of-sample validation

Tested on what it has never seen.

Most backtests only prove a strategy against the data used to build it. This program holds a standing rule: the most recent slice of history is completely off-limits to research. No parameter, no rule, no threshold is ever chosen, tuned, or confirmed by looking at it — it exists only to check, honestly, how the already-finished system performs on data it has never been shown. This split is a fixed methodology check, not a lookback window — it always reflects the complete history below, independent of the performance-window selector above.

Portfolio construction

No single bet carries the book.

Every trading day, the program re-ranks its full universe and concentrates capital only in its strongest-scoring names — capped by sector, so a single theme can never dominate the day's risk. It also has no directional bias: it takes both long and short positions, so it does not depend on markets going up to make money.

Discipline, not prediction

What actually makes it robust.

None of this is theoretical. Every safeguard below is already priced into every figure on this page — not a set of promises layered on top of the numbers, but the reason the numbers look the way they do.

Adapts daily, not once

Every instrument's trading rules are re-selected each day from its own recent, statistically-weighted track record — never fixed at a single historical fit.

Sized by conviction

Position size starts small and scales up only as more of the signal's own conditions confirm — it never grows on a weakening trade.

Exits are mechanical

A revising stop tightens as a trade earns more profit and never loosens — removing the discretion that turns a real winner into a round trip.

Costed like the real market

Realistic exchange fees, market-impact slippage, and every monthly contract rollover are deducted from every trade before any number on this page is calculated — nothing here is a gross, frictionless figure.

Methodology

All figures are the result of a systematic backtest across the strategy's full traded universe (216 NSE F&O-eligible stocks and indices, 212 actually traded), run on hourly-resolution historical data from July 2019 through September 2026, and include realistic NSE transaction charges and an estimated market-impact cost on every trade. Position sizing, capital base (₹25 crore), and margin assumptions are stated as used in the backtest.

This is a backtest, not a live track record

These results are simulated performance, not audited live trading history. A backtest is subject to limitations that live trading is not — including that costs, capacity, and execution quality can differ once real capital is deployed at scale. Past performance, simulated or live, is not indicative of future results.

This page changes only when the underlying research does. Every figure on it traces back to one real backtest, run the same way whether or not anyone is watching.
